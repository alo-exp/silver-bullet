# Research Scope

## Research Question

What is a canonical, evidence-based industry-standard software development process architecture that reflects what high-performing technology organizations actually practice at scale as of mid-2026?

## In Scope

- **18 Process Areas** covering the full software development lifecycle from strategy through retirement
- **Workflow-level detail** within each process area: triggers, steps, decision points, RACI, tool categories, automation opportunities, KPIs, anti-patterns
- **Practice classification**: universal / leading-edge / context-dependent / emerging
- **AI-assisted practices** with credible operational adoption evidence (DORA 2025: 90% of tech professionals use AI at work; GitHub 2024 survey: 97% have used AI tools)
- **Cross-area dependencies** and feedback loops
- **Maturity model** with minimum/standard/leading-edge implementation tiers
- **Role model** and governance implications
- **Context variations**: startup vs enterprise, regulated vs consumer, cloud-native vs hybrid

## Out of Scope

- Vendor-specific product comparisons or endorsements
- Detailed configuration instructions for specific tools
- Academic/theoretical models without industry adoption evidence
- Non-software development domains (hardware, pure IT operations)
- Organization-specific process customization templates

## Key Sources

- DORA Research Program (2018-2025): Longest-running academically rigorous DevOps research
- Google SRE Book & Engineering Practices
- OWASP DevSecOps Guideline
- NIST Secure Software Development Framework (SSDF)
- OpenSSF Projects
- CNCF/OpenTelemetry ecosystem
- Company engineering blogs: Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, Netflix, Spotify, Stripe, GitHub, Atlassian, Shopify, Cloudflare, LinkedIn, Uber, Airbnb
- GitHub Octoverse 2024, Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2024/2025

## Assumptions

1. The target audience is CTO/VP Engineering level adoption planning
2. Organizations have at least some existing SDLC practices
3. Cloud-native and microservices architectures are increasingly dominant but not universal
4. AI-assisted development is now mainstream but adoption maturity varies widely
5. Security and compliance are non-negotiable first-class concerns
6. Developer experience (DevEx) is a recognized competitive advantage
7. Platform engineering has reached mainstream adoption in 2025-2026
