# Research Scope: Canonical SDLC Process Architecture

## Research Question

What is the canonical industry-standard software development process architecture, organized into Process Areas and Workflows, based on best practices used by leading software organizations as of mid-2026?

## Core Components

1. **Process Area Taxonomy** — 18 minimum required areas covering the full SDLC
2. **Workflow Library** — Nested workflows within each process area with detailed attributes
3. **Cross-Area Dependencies** — Feedback loops, integration points, handoff patterns
4. **AI Integration Layer** — Evidence-based AI-assisted/agentic engineering practices
5. **Maturity Model** — Minimum / Standard / Leading-edge implementation tiers
6. **Role Model** — Governance implications for CTO/VP Engineering adoption

## Stakeholder Perspectives

- CTO/VP Engineering — adoption planning, organizational design
- Platform Engineering — IDP design, developer experience
- Engineering Managers — workflow orchestration, team topology
- Individual Contributors — daily practices, tooling, collaboration
- Security/Compliance — governance, risk management
- Product Management — discovery, planning, delivery alignment

## Scope Boundaries

### In Scope

- Practices from Google, Microsoft, Amazon/AWS, Meta, Apple, Netflix, Spotify, Stripe, GitHub, Atlassian, Shopify, Cloudflare, LinkedIn, Uber, Airbnb
- Primary sources: engineering blogs/handbooks, conference talks, DORA, NIST SSDF, OWASP, CNCF/OpenSSF/OpenTelemetry
- Practice classification: universal / leading-edge / context-dependent / emerging
- Security, reliability, quality, DevEx, platform engineering, governance, delivery performance
- AI-assisted/agentic practices with credible operational adoption evidence
- Context variations: startup vs enterprise, regulated vs consumer, B2B vs B2C

### Out of Scope

- Invented process areas without evidence
- Speculative AI practices without operational adoption data
- Proprietary internal-only practices without public documentation
- Legacy waterfall-only processes (covered only as contrast)
- Specific tool recommendations (tool categories only)
- Country-specific regulatory compliance details (general patterns only)

## Success Criteria

1. All 18 required process areas covered with evidence
2. Each process area has: definition, purpose, inputs, workflows, outputs, dependencies, metrics, pitfalls, context variations
3. Each workflow has: name, objective, classification, triggers, steps, roles, tools, outputs, criteria, KPIs, anti-patterns, tiers
4. Minimum 30 high-credibility sources cited
5. Major claims triangulated across 2+ independent sources
6. Suitable for CTO/VP Engineering adoption planning
7. All required artifacts produced (sources.jsonl, evidence.jsonl, claims.jsonl, etc.)

## Key Assumptions

1. "Leading software organizations" refers to companies with >1000 engineers and significant public engineering culture documentation
2. "Mid-2026" practices include publicly documented developments through Q2 2026
3. "Canonical" means widely adopted across multiple organizations, not unique to one
4. AI-assisted practices require evidence of operational adoption, not just announcements
5. Process areas are orthogonal enough to be independently actionable but interconnected enough to form a coherent system

- Full triangulation and critique
